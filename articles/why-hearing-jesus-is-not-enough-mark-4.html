The Parable of the Sower · Mark 4:3–20

Why Hearing Jesus Is Not Enough: Mark 4:3–20

Jesus’ parable of the sower shows why a moment of interest is not the same as lasting faith, and how the word bears fruit in a heart that receives it.

By Matthew Schaefer · Pastor, First Baptist Church of Wellston

You can hear a sermon, agree with it, and still leave unchanged. The words may even move you for a moment. Then pressure comes, daily worries take over, and the teaching seems to disappear. In Mark 4, Jesus explains why hearing alone is not the same as receiving his word. The question is not whether the seed is good. The question is what happens in the ground where it lands.

Jesus makes hearing the issue

Jesus begins with a command: “Listen!” He tells of a sower scattering seed. Some falls on the path, where birds eat it. Some lands on shallow, rocky ground and springs up quickly, only to wither under the sun. Some falls among thorns and is choked. Other seed lands in good soil and produces a harvest.

“He who has ears to hear, let him hear.”

Mark 4:9 (ESV)

The story ends as it began, with hearing. Jesus is not offering a lesson in farming technique. He is pressing his hearers to pay attention to what they do with his teaching. The seed is scattered widely, but it does not bear the same result everywhere. That makes this parable personal. I cannot treat hearing Jesus as a passive act, as though being near his words were the same as receiving them.

The word can be taken away before it takes root

When the disciples ask about the parable, Jesus says that the seed is the word. The seed on the path pictures people who hear, but Satan immediately takes the word away. The message does not settle in them or shape what they do.

“These are the ones along the path, where the word is sown: when they hear, Satan immediately comes and takes away the word that is sown in them.”

Mark 4:15 (ESV)

That warning reaches beyond whether you have access to Scripture or sit through a sermon. You can hear words without attending to them. You can leave with no intention of obeying, and the message can be gone before it has changed anything. I need to ask whether I am actually listening, or simply present while the words pass by.

Jesus does not tell the path to try harder at being soil. The parable exposes the heart’s condition. That makes honest prayer necessary. I can ask God to show me where I am closed to his word and to give me a heart that receives it.

Quick enthusiasm is not the same as deep roots

The rocky ground receives the word with joy. There is a real beginning, but no root. When trouble or persecution comes because of the word, the person falls away. Jesus does not say that hardship creates the shallow root. It reveals it.

“They have no root in themselves, but endure for a while; then, when tribulation or persecution arises on account of the word, immediately they fall away.”

Mark 4:17 (ESV)

A strong first feeling is not proof of lasting faith. I can be moved by a teaching and promise to change, but the question is whether I continue to trust and obey when following Jesus becomes costly. Mark’s wording keeps the cause clear: the trouble arises “on account of the word.” The pressure is not random in the parable. It tests whether the word has taken root.

Good things can crowd out the word

The thorny ground hears the word, but the cares of the world, the deceitfulness of riches, and desires for other things choke it. Jesus names competing loyalties, not just obviously sinful behavior. Work, family, food, and money can matter deeply, yet still take the place of trust and obedience to God.

“The cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches and the desires for other things enter in and choke the word, and it proves unfruitful.”

Mark 4:19 (ESV)

Riches deceive because they promise security that they cannot give. Worries also press for our attention until God’s word has little room to govern our choices. I need to look honestly at what occupies my thoughts and directs my decisions. The question is not simply whether I care about these things, but whether they are choking out the fruit Jesus describes.

The good soil is the person who hears the word, accepts it, and bears fruit. Jesus does not describe fruit as a passing mood or a claim about oneself. It is the result of receiving the word. The harvest differs in measure, but the seed bears fruit in each case.

“Those that were sown on the good soil are the ones who hear the word and accept it and bear fruit, thirtyfold and sixtyfold and a hundredfold.”

Mark 4:20 (ESV)

Ask what your hearing is producing

Jesus gives this parable so his hearers will examine how they receive his word. I can look back over time and ask what fruit has appeared. Have I accepted what Jesus says, or have I only heard it? Does his word continue to shape my response when life presses in? Are worries, money, or other desires crowding it out?

These questions are not a way to earn God’s favor. They are a way to take Jesus seriously. When I see a hard or shallow place in my heart, I can bring it honestly before God and ask for help. The parable does not call me to admire the seed from a distance. It calls me to hear, accept, and bear fruit.
